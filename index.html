Title: CBoard

PCB for managing connections and external sensors in a Lower Limb Prosthesis

This PCB was embedded in a robotic device for rehabilitation: a robotic lower-limb prosthesis. The PCB includes an STM32 microcontroller, peripherals for CAN communication with an EPOS motor driver (Maxon Motors), an amplification circuit for sensing battery level and prosthesis temperature, and I2C and UART buses for communication with external sensors (sEMG, IMUs, force sensors, etc.). In addition, a buck regulator was used to power all the circuits, and it was designed to meet EMI emission limits for electromagnetic compatibility (EMC).

Details: 4-layer PCB manufactred and assembled in JLCPCB.

Designed with KiCAD

Title: MBoard

PCB for capturing 8 strain gauge-based force sensors

This circuit was developed to capture signals from force sensors based on strain gauges arranged in a Wheatstone bridge. Each channel includes a 2nd-order analog low-pass filter for anti-aliasing, connected to an instrumentation amplifier that amplifies the signal and effectively rejects common-mode noise. In addition to the instrumentation amplifiers, 16-bit ADCs were used to digitize the signal directly on this PCB. Together with the CBoard, this PCB was installed in a robotic lower-limb prosthesis.

Details: 4-layer PCB manufactred and assembled in JLCPCB.

Designed with KiCAD

Title: MyoSym

sEMG Module with 3 bipolar channels

sEMG acquisition module containing 3 bipolar channels to be connected. Each channel is routed to a 24-bit ADC for high-resultion without the need to apply a hige gain stage. Anti-aliasing filters were used in the analog front-end. A STM32 chip was used to process the signals with digital filters, and send them to the USB Bus or via SPI to an external micro SD card. A fourth channel in the PCB was used as Right-Leg Drive to reduce noise in each sEMG channel.

Details: 4-layer PCB manufactred and assembled in JLCPCB.

Designed with KiCAD

Title: EMGBoard

sEMG system: an evolution from MyoSym

This board is a update from the previous version of the MyoSym. This time, a more robust analog front-end was created using intrumentation amplifiers before the 24-bit ADC. Also, a more compact STM32 was used, together with a Galvanic Isolator between the MCU and the ADC to guarantee patient/user isolation. This board was used to collect EMG data in two main projects in a Research Lab: Lower limb exoskeleton for people with Cerebral Palsy and a Lower-limb prosthesis.

Details: 4-layer PCB manufactred and assembled in JLCPCB.

Designed with KiCAD

Title: Motor Shield

Motor Driver PCB

This PCB was developed in order to control a Motor Driver (ESCON2 from Maxon Motors). It contains connections for the Encoder, Hall Sensors and Power (24V - 48V). Also, it contains a USB connection so a Master controller can command the torque, velocity and position of the actuator (motor + transmission). A force sensor acquisiton circuit is also implemented in the PCB. This project is being used to control the 6 actuators in a lower limb exoskeleton (hip, knee, and ankle, from each leg).

Details: 4-layer PCB manufactred and assembled in JLCPCB.

Designed with KiCAD
